## Candidate identity and activation

The existing bearer principal establishes candidate and Telegram user through verified signed Telegram launch data. Neither destination nor candidate ID is accepted in a notification request. Persist the exact composite identity already enforced by `telegram_identities`.

A candidate first saves an explicit opt-in in Settings. Connecting delivery is a separate action: a POST creates a 32-byte random, ten-minute, single-use activation challenge bound to candidate, Telegram user, current bot credential scope and consenting session. Store only its hash. Return an HTTPS `t.me/<configured_bot_username>?start=notify_<base64url>` link; the bot username is validated server configuration. This payload fits Telegram's documented 64-character start-parameter limit. No raw challenge or launch data is logged or retained in evidence.

Extend the parsed Telegram update with chat type and message time. The existing authenticated Bot API getUpdates path is the only source of activation updates. Accept only an ordinary message with chat.type=private, chat.id=from.id>0, non-bot sender, current admission, exact expected Telegram user, unexpired/unconsumed challenge and credential scope. Reject groups, channels, forwarded activation commands and absent/malformed identity evidence. A forwarded/leaked token cannot bind a different user. Consume challenge and activate its candidate identity atomically under the candidate lock. A repeated update cannot reset consent or baseline.

Activation permits delivery but does not itself enable consent. Blocked/unavailable delivery is suspended. Reconnection requires a new user activation and explicit consent if it was revoked; it never silently resumes old backlog. `/notifications_off` is an exact new candidate-scoped stop command. It is routed before legacy authorization, consumes only verified private identity and atomically disables consent/cancels unsent work. All existing owner commands/callbacks continue through their current dispatcher. No candidate workflow mutation is introduced in Telegram messages.

## Consent and preferences

Persist candidate-owned preferences with optimistic revision and stable mutation request ID. Defaults: enabled=false, paused=false, immediate=true, digest=false, minimumScore=85, timezone=UTC, quietStart/quietEnd=null, eligibilityPolicy=ELIGIBLE_ONLY. Timezone must be a valid IANA ZoneId, not an offset or invented browser value. Score is an integer 0–100. Quiet hours require both HH:mm values; equal start/end is invalid. At least one delivery mode is required when enabled. Copy the browser's timezone only after the user selects/saves it.

The only initial eligibility policy is ELIGIBLE_ONLY. UNKNOWN and INELIGIBLE never send, regardless of score or coverage. Show the policy in plain language in Settings; no inference that a high score establishes eligibility. Do not add a user-facing evidence-coverage control. Put the required simple minimum-score field under optional advanced notification controls; no matching weights or model parameters are exposed.

Consent records its revision, accepted policy version, timestamp, authorizing session hash and current credential scope. Background delivery does not require a 15-minute Mini App session to remain unexpired; otherwise Telegram could not function proactively. Explicit revocation of the authorizing session, disappearance/change of its identity, invite removal, beta closure, credential-epoch/token rotation, pause, disabled consent or recovery blocks future sends. Re-enabling after revocation requires fresh authenticated consent and a new baseline. An old activation challenge cannot revive it.

All notification mutations lock candidate, revalidate the principal and check expected revision after acquiring the lock. Revocation participates in that same lock. Pause, disable, profile activation, mode/threshold/policy changes and reconnect cancel unsent rows from the superseded generation. They preserve immutable attempt history. Resume deliberately establishes a fresh baseline, so absence never creates a catch-up flood. An opt-out cannot recall a message already accepted by Telegram; the concurrency tests specify the send/opt-out ordering.

## Subscription baseline and flood prevention

Do not use computed_at, search-profile creation, fact bootstrap, result backfill or legacy CREATED admission as evidence that a vacancy is new. The legacy CREATED outcome can also mean promotion of an existing catalogue-only vacancy.

V20 adds a first-discovery event only on an actual new shared job insert in the normal ingestion transaction. Existing jobs at V20 migration get no discovery event. Fact bootstrap, extractor upgrades, historical backfill and later owner admission cannot create one. Use the existing commit-ordered catalogue revision/barrier to record discovery ordering; do not use a sequence that can commit out of order.

On enabled consent plus activation, capture the committed catalogue watermark under its existing publication lock as baseline. The later of those two prerequisites establishes the subscription. Every pause/resume, profile revision or consent/preferences generation change takes a new baseline and invalidates queued work. Profile edits cannot turn historical rows into notifications.

Enqueue only when all are true: the vacancy's true first-discovery event is strictly after the subscription baseline; the discovery is at most 24 hours old; the current completed profile belongs to the candidate; the exact current result meets eligibility, score policy; the candidate has never logically consumed this job notification. A previously UNKNOWN/ineligible new vacancy may become eligible within this 24-hour discovery window, without changing baseline/profile generation; a later changed match for old catalogue content never sends. A delivered vacancy is never resent just because score, profile, facts or semantics changed.

Use a lifetime unique logical identity `(candidate_id, job_id)` across immediate and digest modes, consent generations and all result revisions. Preserve canceled/dropped/uncertain records as audit history. Repeated match publication uses ON CONFLICT and cannot create a second logical recommendation. There is no historical recommendation import button in this phase.

Per candidate: at most 100 active pending outbox rows; excess eligible discoveries are recorded as DROPPED with a capacity reason and remain visible in Phase 4 matching/Mini App. At most three immediate messages per local day, at least one minute apart; optional one daily digest with at most ten previously unsent vacancies. The digest does not repeat immediate items. With both modes enabled, immediate gets the first three, digest can include up to ten others. Excess eligible items expire/drop with a recorded reason, rather than rolling into an unlimited future backlog. Total personalized recommendation messages are at most four per candidate/local date. Timezone changes also retain rolling 24-hour caps, preventing repeated budget reset. Discovery backlog beyond 24 hours expires even during outage/quiet hours; matching may legitimately finish too late to alert. Document this availability tradeoff.

## Outbox, claims and external delivery

V20 introduces preferences/consent state, activation challenge state, verified delivery identity, true vacancy-discovery events, candidate/job logical outbox rows, candidate/day digest envelopes and immutable delivery attempts. Composite FKs enforce candidate/session/Telegram identity and candidate/job/result ownership. States and field bounds are checked by PostgreSQL. Existing rows and V1–V19 bytes/checksums remain unchanged. Index pending claims by state, next_attempt_at and candidate; keep queries bounded.

Producer admission runs after successful current matching publication and also has a bounded reconciliation path for interrupted admission. It uses current result pointers and explicit discovery events, never global scores. Publication must not acquire candidate locks after holding the catalogue lock; preserve the current candidate→work→fact lock order. Discovery recording never fans out synchronously over all candidates. Reconciliation uses a durable bounded cursor per subscription, not repeated full-catalogue scans.

Worker phases: PENDING/RETRY → CLAIMED (lease and fencing token) → SENDING (durable attempt boundary) → SENT, RETRY, CANCELED, FAILED or UNCERTAIN. Use transactional FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED with batch≤25 and finite attempts≤5. Retry only a known negative response such as a valid Telegram 429 with bounded retry_after; definitive 400/403 marks unavailable/blocked and pauses identity as applicable. Do not repeatedly retry permanent bad requests. Configuration/authentication errors halt personalized delivery without leaking token-bearing URLs.

The existing shared `ExternalHttpClient.postJson` retries POST after timeout/IO/5xx. Do not use it for this sender. Add a narrowly scoped single-attempt Telegram transport with fixed api.telegram.org HTTPS origin, no redirects, finite connect/response/body bounds, HTML escaping and sanitized typed results. Parse successful message_id for audit, documented flood retry_after for scheduling, blocked/chat-unavailable errors for suspension. Transport exceptions retain no raw URL, token, body or cause.

A timeout, connection reset after dispatch, malformed success, HTTP 5xx with uncertain acceptance, process crash after SENDING or a database failure after external acceptance is UNCERTAIN. Do not automatically resend uncertain attempts, including after lease expiry/restart. A conservative hold may lose a notification that was never sent; automatic retry could duplicate a private message. Telegram sendMessage provides no application idempotency key, so exactly-once delivery is not promised. Retain uncertainty and expose a non-sensitive pause/reconciliation reason. No routine user button blindly retries uncertain sends.

A crash before the durable SENDING boundary leaves CLAIMED work reclaimable. A crash afterward leaves a held attempt even if the HTTP call had not actually started. Commit SENDING before dispatch; reject expired tokens. During the final validation/send window, serialize candidate preference/profile/revocation changes and relevant job/fact changes with a consistent lock order and bounded network deadline. Update sender completion only for its current fenced attempt. Cancellation that commits before this window prevents dispatch; cancellation waiting behind an already-dispatched send takes effect after it. Test both orderings and document the irreducible external side-effect race.

Immediately before dispatch revalidate: current invited account/identity/credential scope; unrevoked consent; verified private chat; active completed search-profile revision; exact current result pointer/job/fact/extractor/scorer identity; ELIGIBLE; score; job OPEN/not expired; baseline/discovery age; mode, quiet hours, date/rate budgets; no sent/uncertain/foreign history; current lease/fence; non-recovery/lifecycle gate. A stale row is canceled, never rendered with a global fallback. For digest, each item independently revalidates; render one bounded immutable attempt envelope and associate every included logical identity with it. No empty digest is sent.

## Approved concurrency refinement — exact lock graph

The user's approval authorizes this detailed plan and local implementation without another scope/plan approval round. No commit or publication is authorized. The owner path remains legacy-exclusive. Future twice-daily 18:00/00:00 delivery is explicitly not fulfilled by Phase 5; Phase 5 digest is 09:00 local only. All notification limits affect delivery selection, never the matching records or Mini App recommendations.

Use these lock ranks; all multi-row sets are acquired in ascending primary-key order. No routine holding a later-ranked lock may acquire an earlier rank. PostgreSQL reads without FOR UPDATE are MVCC observations, not lock acquisition in this graph.

| Rank | Resource | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Per-bot poll session advisory key | Dedicated connection, pg_try_advisory_lock only; released in finally/connection close. Poll path never takes sending gate. |
| 1 | Per-bot personalized sending transaction advisory key | pg_try_advisory_xact_lock only; contention skips, never waits. Distinct namespace from polling. |
| 2 | Candidate row | Every consent mutation, session revoke, profile activation, notification admission and dispatch starts with this row; worker selection uses FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED on candidate. One candidate per notification or matching-initialization transaction; the existing initializer is refactored into bounded one-candidate transactions. |
| 3 | Matching work state, then notification preference row | Existing matching publication keeps candidate→matching state; notification code never locks matching state. Preference writes and final dispatch share candidate serialization. |
| 4 | Job rows, ascending job ID | Final send locks at most ten jobs with FOR UPDATE, before any fact/current-result locks. Ingestion already locks job before fact state. |
| 5 | Fact-state rows, ascending job ID | Matching publication locks candidate→work→fact state, but never job after fact. Final send follows candidate→job→fact. Immutable fact/result history is read, never updated/locked for publication. |
| 6 | Candidate current-result pointers, ascending job ID | Only final validation needs locked current pointers; matching publication already holds candidate, so candidate serialization excludes competing same-candidate pointer publication. Read exact joined pointer/profile/fact/semantic versions. |
| 7 | Notification outbox logical rows, ascending job ID; attempt/envelope state | Claim/admission/cancellation mutate rows only under candidate lock. Immediate and digest claims use the same outbox identity and candidate serialization. No worker selects outbox FOR UPDATE and then waits for candidate. |
| 8 | Catalogue publication barrier | Only ingestion (job→fact→catalogue) and baseline transactions (candidate→preference→catalogue) take this lock. Never acquire candidate/job/fact/outbox after taking catalogue barrier. Profile activation captures baseline as its final database-locking step. |

Lock graph justification: matching is 2→3→5, ingestion 4→5→8, baseline 2→3→7→8, sending 1→2→3→4→5→6→7. No participating routine has a reverse edge. Admission reads committed discovery events and matching pointers while holding candidate; it never locks catalogue. Baseline watermark locks the publication barrier as the last step, so events are ordered by commit rather than sequence allocation. Fact bootstrap never creates notification discoveries. Use a transaction-mandatory new-job discovery hook only when the shared job was actually inserted, not the legacy CREATED promotion outcome. No trigger locks candidates from ingestion/catalogue operations.

All personalized worker/mutation transactions set local lock_timeout=1000ms, statement_timeout=2000ms and idle_in_transaction_session_timeout=12000ms; TransactionTemplate timeout=15 seconds. Existing profile/revoke transactions joining this boundary apply the same bounds before taking candidate. Lock failures abort before dispatch or yield a safe retry of a claim, not a retry of an attempted external send. Sending uses a single HTTP attempt, connect timeout two seconds, whole async exchange deadline five seconds including bounded body consumption (16 KiB), cancellation on deadline and sanitized outcomes. No network call occurs before the durable SENDING marker. No ordinary blocking advisory lock is used. Bound pending candidate selection and discovery scan to 25/100 rows per tick respectively. Recovery checks precede any lock/write/network work.

Atomic arbitration: candidate lock owns both mode choice and date/rate budget. A unique `(candidate_id,job_id)` outbox row is lifetime identity, with a composite owned-result FK; it survives cancellation/uncertainty. Immediate and digest select from that row set in one transaction and assign one immutable attempt/envelope ID before committing SENDING. Digest slot identity is `(candidate_id,local_date)` and rolling daily budgets also apply. A second worker cannot claim those rows, even across processes. Retries after definitive 429 retain the lifetime logical identities and use a fresh claim fence, and reserve budgets only as specified; ambiguous outcomes consume the attempt budget and never return to pending automatically.

Dispatch protocol: preparation commits SENDING and immutable attempt input. A second transaction obtains try sending gate, candidate, preference and sorted job/fact/current/outbox locks; revalidates consent/session scope, profile and exact result/currentness, availability/age/baseline and schedule; performs one bounded HTTP call while holding those locks; commits typed outcome for that attempt only. Revocation/profile mutation that wins candidate first prevents the send; one waiting behind dispatch becomes effective after the already-started side effect. If the sender crashes after SENDING, that durable row remains held and is treated as uncertain, never automatically reclaimed. Recovery and disarmed startup leave it unchanged; they do not rewrite SENDING to UNCERTAIN. Crash before SENDING remains reclaimable. A failure after external acceptance leaves committed SENDING and is treated as uncertain even when the outcome-write transaction rolls back. A replacement worker never dispatches an existing SENDING attempt. The process that created the attempt holds its local claim identity and starts final dispatch at most once.

Database rewind: add a persisted V20 notification runtime epoch plus a fresh random in-memory instance nonce on every application startup. Dispatch starts DISARMED on every process, including a normal restart. Require a private external approval file whose epoch and instance nonce exactly match this live process; never accept a reusable environment boolean as reconciliation proof. The operator writes that approval only after verifying retained external-delivery history. A restored database and an old approval file cannot arm a newly started process because its nonce is new. Reconciliation can withhold approval indefinitely without deleting queued records. After restart, known pending work can resume after approval and full revalidation; every old SENDING attempt remains held uncertain. PostgreSQL alone cannot infer arbitrary rewind, so never claim automatic rewind detection. A restore underneath a running app requires stopping it first; the runbook must enforce that normal maintenance boundary. ## Final Task 2 implementation refinements — 21 October 2026

These refinements supersede the earlier vague producer retry and delivery fairness descriptions. They remain within the approved local Phase 5 scope.

Admission scans at most 100 commit-ordered matching_catalogue_changes per candidate transaction, strictly forward from the preference event_cursor. The upper bound is the current candidate_matching_state published event_cursor only when the matching state is READY and owns the exact completed active profile and current extractor/scorer semantics. It never advances past unpublished matching progress and never periodically resets to rescan the catalogue. Every candidate item must also have a true insertion discovery strictly after the subscription baseline and within 24 hours. A fresh UNKNOWN vacancy can become eligible after a facts change because that change emits a later catalogue event and matching publication advances the barrier after publishing the exact new current result. Historical/bootstrap-only changes have no true discovery and cannot enqueue.

A 09:00 digest deferred by quiet hours into the next calendar date is eligible when that quiet period ends on the actual current date. The scheduler considers yesterday's target only when that target's explicit quiet-hours deferral crosses into today. It never catches up an ordinary missed prior-day digest. Candidate/local actual-date envelope uniqueness and rolling 24-hour budgets still apply. Thus 08:00–07:00 quiet hours permit one deferred morning digest between 07:00 and 08:00 instead of permanently suppressing digest delivery.

Delivery arbitration uses a separate durable notification_preferences.last_delivery_check ordinal, allocated from notification_delivery_checks and indexed with candidate_id. A checked candidate, including a quiet or budget-limited candidate, commits a new ordinal and moves behind other due candidates. Producer last_scanned_at/event_cursor remain independent. This avoids starvation at the 1000-invite bound and does not depend on wall-clock monotonicity. One candidate is locked with FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED per transaction. Sequence allocation takes no participating table-row lock, so it adds no reverse edge to the rank graph. Sequence gaps after rollback do not affect correctness. A 60-second next_attempt_at deferral remains a polling-efficiency bound; it is not the fairness mechanism.

Digest rendering preserves full valid public HTTP(S) vacancy links, escapes public excerpts and includes only complete cards fitting one 4096 UTF-16 envelope. At most 10 selected items can enter one attempt. Every rendered member has an immutable notification_attempt_items row owning its candidate/job/result/generation/fence. Selected items omitted because they are stale, have an invalid public link or cannot fit are terminally dropped with a reason; matching/Mini App visibility remains available. No untracked split sends or URL-shortening service is introduced.

The existing poller also receives candidate activation updates when personalized notifications alone are enabled, while the explicit legacy chat/user pair is authorized whenever the original poller is enabled (`commandsEnabled || review enabled`). Personalized-only polling cannot authorize retained legacy IDs when both original switches are false. The private review-bot authorization path remains independent. Malformed configured bot tokens produce a disabled personalized transport and a constant sanitized HALT outcome before HTTP; they cannot leak through URI-construction exceptions. Activation is independent of the external delivery approval file, while recovery still rejects activation/polling/background delivery.
